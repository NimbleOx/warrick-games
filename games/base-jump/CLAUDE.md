# Base Jump

A base-capture game like *Modern Conflict* (iPad), with ideas from *Galcon* and *Mushroom Wars*, played in real time or in simultaneous turns. The map is a set of bases joined by roads. Every base you hold builds units. Send them out to take more bases: tanks have to drive along the roads, helicopters fly straight to any base. Take every base, or hold the most when time (or the last turn) runs out.

Read `README.md` for how it plays, how it's built and how to run it; this file is the rules and design the code keeps to.

## Rules for v1

- 2–8 players online, each on their own device. Drag from one of your bases to any other base to send units there; drag across several of your bases first to send from all of them. Or tap a base of yours, then tap the target. Tap empty ground or press Esc to clear the selection.
- Send size: 25%, 50% (default) or all of each selected base's units, picked in the HUD or with 1, 2, 3. A key or button for "select all my bases" (A).
- Two kinds of base, shown by their icon: **depots** build tanks, **airfields** build helicopters. A base holds both kinds if they arrive there.
- **Tanks** drive along roads only. A convoy can pass through any number of your own bases on the way, by the quickest road route. If a base on its route has changed hands by the time it gets there, the convoy stops and attacks it. If there's no road route through your bases, tanks can't go.
- **Helicopters** fly straight to any base, nearly twice as fast as tanks.
- When you send to a base, each selected base sends its tanks by road (if it can) and its helicopters by air. If it can do neither, the game says why. While aiming, the map shows each route, how many units would go, and whether that's enough against what's there now.
- Combat when units reach a base that isn't yours: like fights like first (tanks against tanks, helicopters against helicopters), then the rest. **One tank is worth two helicopters** in a fight, attacking or defending. The attackers take the base if any survive the garrison; a tie leaves the defender holding an empty base. Units arriving at your own base join its garrison. Units on the move don't fight each other.
- Production: each player's base builds one unit of its kind every so often, faster at higher levels, until it holds its cap (more units can still arrive). Neutral bases build nothing.
- Decay, from Mushroom Wars: a base holding more than its cap loses a twentieth of the excess (at least one unit) every second. Without it, CPU free-for-alls ended with two empires stacking thousands of units on their fronts and trading bases forever.
- Levels 1–3, shown by the base's size and the dots above it. Upgrading spends units in that base (15 for level 2, 30 for level 3) and raises its build rate and cap. Select one base and press Upgrade, or U. The button shows the units against the cost (15/30) and stays clickable, so pressing it early says why it can't be done yet.
- Start: everyone gets one level-2 depot with 15 tanks. Every other base is neutral with a garrison of tanks (bigger at higher levels). Every map has at least one airfield per player.
- A player with no bases and nothing on the move is out. The last one left wins. With a time limit (host picks 5, 10 or 15 minutes, or none; default 10), the most bases when time runs out wins, then the most units.
- Two modes, picked by the host (real time by default). The lobby shows only the current mode's limits.
- **Turns** (simultaneous, like *Frozen Synapse*): everyone plans at once, hidden from each other, then the battle runs 5 seconds (`TURN_TICKS`) and everyone plans again. The battle's rules don't change; only when orders go in does. Sending and upgrading add to your plan. Each order is checked against what your plan so far leaves, the map shows the plan as arrows with when each lands ("this turn", "next turn", "in 3 turns"), and your bases show what's left. Undo (Backspace) takes back the last order. Done (Enter) says you're ready; changing your plan takes it back. The turn runs once every player still in is done, or when time to plan runs out (host picks 15, 30 or 60 seconds, or no limit; default 30). Once the only players still planning have dropped out, they get 15 seconds (`AWAY_MS`) to come back, shown on the clock, and then the turn runs without them: a reload or a phone switching apps mustn't end anyone's turn, and nobody waits forever for someone who has gone. The round ends at the turn limit (25, 40 or 60 turns, or none; default 40), or by conquest. There's no countdown: turn 1's planning is the start.
- Leaving mid-game makes your bases neutral and recalls your units. Disconnected players keep their bases; they keep building.
- CPU players (easy, normal, hard). In turns they plan the moment planning starts and are always ready; they look once a turn, so normal and hard make more attacks in that look, and every level sizes attacks for what the target will hold when they arrive (`TURN_STYLES`). Easy attacks the odd neighbour it can beat and doesn't defend. Normal picks targets by value against cost and travel time (by road or by air), reinforces bases under attack, moves units up from behind the front, and upgrades safe bases. Hard thinks about twice as often, goes all in from front-line bases when a capture needs it, gangs bases up on one target, and leans on the leader. Hard beats normal about 4 duels in 5; normal beats easy every time (README). Caution made CPUs weaker in every test, so keep that in mind when tuning.
- Not in v1: special powers (Modern Conflict's air strike), more unit types (artillery, missiles), fog of war, hand-made maps, teams. Design so they can be added.

## Design notes

- Map generation (`src/shared/map.ts`), seeded: scatter bases with best-candidate sampling, so they're spread out but not on a grid. Roads are the relative neighbourhood graph (connected, and it never crosses itself) plus a share of the extra Gabriel-graph edges, so there are loops and choices. Drop any road that passes too close to another base. Then pick the starting bases with farthest-point sampling over road distance, set the neutrals' kinds, levels and garrisons, and score the map for fairness (how many and how valuable the bases closest to each start are). Make several candidates and keep the fairest.
- The simulation (`src/shared/battle.ts`) is deterministic and integer-only: production counts in thousandths of a unit per tick, travel times are whole ticks fixed when units leave. Convoy and helicopter positions between bases are worked out from the departure and arrival ticks, so nothing moves in the state except at waypoints.
- Real-time like Turf War: the Durable Object steps the battle 10 times a second while a round is on and someone is connected. Players' commands (send, upgrade) are checked when they arrive (so mistakes come back as an error at once), queued, and applied at the start of the next tick. CPU commands go through the same path.
- Turns reuse all of that. While everyone plans, the tick loop is off and the room's alarm holds the deadline (`timerAt`). Plans live in memory (`Live.plans`), go back to their own player as a `plan` message (also after a hello), and go into the queue all at once when the turn runs, so the run's first frame carries every order and clients replay it like any other frame. Players' plans can't touch each other (orders only change the player's own bases, and routes only pass through their own bases), so applying them one player after another is the same as all at once; only the order of same-tick arrivals depends on it, which is why the first player rotates each turn. The state's `turn.tick` is where the plan is made: a client only lets you plan when its copy of the battle is there. A deadline the room noticed long after it passed (nobody was here) starts over instead of running the turn. A player who leaves sends everyone a fresh snapshot.
- Every tick the server broadcasts one frame: the tick number and the commands applied that tick. Clients run the same code on their copy of the battle, so the frame stays tiny. Every 50 ticks a frame carries a checksum; a mismatch or a gap makes the client ask for a snapshot. Snapshots (the map plus every base and group) go out at round start, in the welcome, and on request.
- The round lives in memory. A deploy restarts the Durable Object, and the first player back finds the battle gone: in real time the round starts over on a new map (the tick loop keeps the object awake all round, so only a restart loses it). A round in turns lasts much longer, and nothing keeps the object awake while everyone plans, so it can also hibernate then. So the room saves it: the battle as each turn's planning begins ("saved": round, turn, snapshot) and the plans whenever they change ("plans"), through `changedKeys`, and forgets both when the round is over. Whenever an event finds the round gone, `makeGame` brings it back (`restore`): while planning, nothing is lost; mid-run, the turn plays again from its start with the same orders (the battle is deterministic, so it plays out the same), and the first hello says so. Anyone who left since leaves the restored battle too. Clients take a snapshot that's behind their copy as a rewind, dropping their feed and plan.
- Rendering: canvas, roads first, then units in transit, then bases. The map is laid out landscape; on a portrait screen the client swaps x and y, so it fills a phone held upright. Hit-testing uses the same transform.
